# Yomu: product one-pager

**What it is:** Paste a line of Japanese and read it word by word: each word
with its furigana, how it is said and how it is spelled in romaji, what it
means, which kanji it uses, and the sounds and grammar that make it behave the
way it does.

**Who it is for:** an adult teaching themselves Japanese who met a line
somewhere (a sign, a lyric, a message from a friend, a sentence in a graded
reader) and wants to check how it is said and what each word is, with no
teacher to ask. They learned English by reading and sounding words out, c-a-r
then car, so that is the move Yomu makes: the beats of a word one by one, then
the whole word. Romaji is a complement to the kana, never a replacement: it is
there to check the ear against, and it can be switched off.

**Register:** product (a tool someone uses), not brand (a page someone visits).

**The scene:** evening, at a desk or on a sofa, a laptop or a phone in hand and
the line they met open in another app; they paste it, look for a minute or
five, sound a word out, and go back to what they were reading. Dark ground,
because it sits next to other reading after dark, and a reading column,
because the text is the thing.

**What done looks like:** they can say the line aloud, correctly timed, and
know what each word in it is: its dictionary form, its meaning, and the one
sound or grammar point that would have tripped them up.

**My kanji:** the one thing Yomu keeps for later. A kanji met in a line can be
saved from the kanji table or the Word panel, and the reading marks the ones
not saved yet, so a familiar text shows at a glance what is still new. Every
kanji also keeps a count of the texts it was met in, which says which ones
are worth saving: the screen lists the most met that are not. A review is the
smallest that works: the kanji, a moment to recall, the answer with the words
it was met in, then Again or Got it on a doubling schedule. It is there so a
kanji met on Monday is still known on Friday, not to turn the reader into a
course.

**What it is not:** a translator (DeepL and Google Translate are one click
away, and the text goes there only on that click), a course, or a flashcard
app: My kanji reviews only what the learner chose to keep, from what they
read. Nothing typed here leaves the browser. What is kept is the last text,
until Clear, and My kanji (kanji, counts and up to eight dictionary words
each, never the text), until Clear all.
